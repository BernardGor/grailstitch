Few vintage garments hold their value—or provoke as much debate among collectors—as a pair of 1960s or 1970s Levi’s 501 jeans. Between 1965 and 1979, Levi Strauss & Co. quietly changed its looms, stitching machines, rivets, and labeling systems multiple times. Learning to read those small construction details is the difference between overpaying for an early-1980s pair and spotting a genuine 1960s grail in a dimly lit thrift rack.

## 1. The Red Tab: "Big E" vs. "Small e"

The fastest dating clue sits on the right rear pocket. Until **1971**, Levi’s stitched its name in all-capital letters (**LEVI’S**) on the red rayon tab. Collectors call these **"Big E"** pairs.

- **Pre-1971 ("Big E"):** Look for a capital **E** in *LEVI’S*. Pairs from the late 1960s through 1971 typically feature double-sided tabs (the name appears on both sides of the loop) and lack care instructions inside the waistband.
- **1971 and Later ("Small e"):** Starting in 1971, Levi’s switched to lowercase letters (**LeVI’S**). Early 1970s "Small e" pairs still retain classic 1960s tailoring and selvedge denim, making them far more valuable than later 1980s jeans.

## 2. Check the Outseam for Red-Line Selvedge

Until roughly **1983–1984**, Levi’s wove 501 denim on narrow shuttle looms, creating a self-finished fabric edge known as **selvedge** (or *self-edge*). Turn the jeans inside out and inspect the outer leg seam:

- **Red-Line Selvedge:** You should see two clean white edges bound with a thin red (or pink-faded) thread running down the entire outseam.
- **Overlocked Seams:** If the raw fabric edge is wrapped in standard serger thread without a clean woven selvedge strip, the jeans were almost certainly made after 1984.

## 3. Hidden Rivets vs. Bar Tacks (The 1966 Transition)

Before **1966**, Levi’s reinforced the upper corners of the back pockets with concealed copper rivets hidden beneath the denim—a detail that notoriously scratched wooden chairs and leather saddles.

- **Pre-1966:** Feel the upper corners of the rear pockets; you will detect hard metal rivets underneath the fabric, and the pockets will lack black thread reinforcement.
- **1966–Present ("Bar Tack" Era):** In 1966, Levi’s replaced hidden rivets with heavy black thread stitching called **bar tacks**. A pair with a **Big E** tab *and* black bar tacks narrows your production window immediately to **1966–1971**.

## 4. Back Pocket Stitching: Single Stitch vs. Chain Stitch

Flip the jeans inside out and examine the top edge of the back pockets:

- **1960s to Mid-1970s (Single Stitch):** The top hem of the back pocket is sewn with a single straight line of thread. Collectors refer to early-1970s "Small e" pairs with this feature as **"66 Single Stitch"** 501s—one of the sweet spots for vintage wearers.
- **Circa 1977 Onward (Chain Stitch):** Around 1977, Levi’s upgraded its machines to a looped **chain stitch** along the top of the back pockets, coinciding with the "66 Chain Stitch" era.

## 5. Inside Care Tags and Pocket Bag Stamps

Before the early 1970s, Levi’s did not sew fabric care tags inside the jeans; washing instructions were printed directly onto the white cotton front pocket bag.

- **Printed Pocket Bags (Late 1960s–Early 1970s):** Look for faded black ink stamping on the interior pocket lining.
- **Small White Paper/Fabric Care Tag (1971–1984):** Located along the left hip seam inside the jeans. Look for the factory code stamped onto the back of the top waist button (such as **6**, **2**, or **524**) and match it to the numeric code printed on the bottom line of the care tag.

## Quick Dating Checklist Before You Buy

1. **Read the Red Tab:** Capital **E** means 1971 or earlier; lowercase **e** means 1971 or later.
2. **Check the Outseam:** Confirm a true woven red-line selvedge edge.
3. **Inspect Back Pocket Corners:** Hidden metal rivets = pre-1966; black thread bar tacks = 1966 or later.
4. **Look Inside the Back Pocket Top:** Single straight stitch = pre-1977; looped chain stitch = circa 1977 or later.
5. **Measure Flat:** Vintage 501s were shrink-to-fit (*XX* denim) and almost always measure 1 to 2 inches smaller in the waist and inseam than the paper patch indicates.
