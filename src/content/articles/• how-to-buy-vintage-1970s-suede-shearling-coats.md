Nothing captures 1970s heritage outerwear quite like a heavyweight **suede and shearling coat**. From Western ranch coats and B-3 flight jackets to tailored European double-breasted coats, authentic 1970s sheepskin outerwear remains in high demand every autumn and winter. However, buying 50-year-old leather and wool online carries real risks: synthetic acrylic linings often masquerade as genuine shearling, and improperly stored hides can suffer from irreversible leather dry rot.

## 1. Real Shearling vs. Synthetic "Sherpa" Pile

The single biggest pricing mistake buyers make is confusing **true shearling** with **faux shearling (acrylic Sherpa)**:

- **Inspect the Backing (The Parting Test):** Genuine shearling is tanned from a single sheepskin hide with the wool still attached to the skin. If you part the wool fibers down to the base, you will see **smooth, natural leather skin**—never a woven fabric mesh or knitted polyester grid.
- **Burn & Touch Characteristics:** Real wool fleece feels dense, slightly matte, and naturally lanolin-rich without plastic shine. Synthetic acrylic pile from the 1970s often looks overly uniform, feels coarse or "crunchy" at the tips, and matting tends to clump into shiny synthetic knots.
- **No Separate Lining Seam:** On a true double-face shearling coat, the interior wool *is* the reverse side of the exterior suede, so the coat does not have a loose hanging lining sewn separately at the hem.

## 2. How to Spot Leather Dry Rot Before You Buy

Fifty-year-old hides stored in dry attics or damp basements lose their natural oils over time, leading to **dry rot**—a structural breakdown where leather tears like damp cardboard:

- **Check High-Stress Seam Lines:** Ask the seller for macro photos of the **underarm seams, shoulder tops, and buttonholes**. Dry-rotted suede shows tiny hairline cracks, flaking surface dust, or dark brittle edges where thread pulls through the hide.
- **The Pinch & Flex Test:** Healthy 1970s suede bends softly and recovers without creasing sharply. If the seller mentions the leather feels *"stiff," "crispy,"* or *"fragile,"* avoid the coat—no amount of leather conditioner can restore dried-out collagen fibers once they begin tearing.
- **Hardware & Zipper Clues:** Authentic 1970s American shearling and suede coats typically feature heavy brass **Talon**, **Scovill**, or **YKK** zippers, or woven **leather football buttons** (braided leather knot buttons).

## 3. Top 1970s Heritage Labels Worth Searching For

While unbranded 1970s coats can still offer great quality, several heritage makers consistently command higher collector value and superior hide tanning:

- **Sawyer of Napa (California):** Widely regarded as the gold standard of 1970s American shearling coats, featuring butter-soft Napa sheepskin and meticulous whipstitched seams.
- **Schott NYC & Golden Bear:** Iconic American outerwear makers whose 1970s suede truckers, ranch coats, and B-3 shearling bomber jackets feature heavy-duty hardware and accurate sizing.
- **Pioneer Wear & Jo-Kay (New Mexico):** Classic Southwestern makers known for 1970s roughout suede coats, horn buttons, and authentic Western yoke stitching.

## 4. Vintage 1970s Sizing and Shoulder Fit

1970s outerwear was tailored noticeably trimmer through the chest and shoulders than 1990s coats:

- **Narrower Armholes:** Expect higher, closer-fitting armholes designed to layer over a thin knit sweater or Western shirt rather than a thick modern hoodie.
- **Measure Across the Back Shoulders:** Because shearling wool adds 0.5 to 1 inch of interior thickness, a tagged **Size 40 (Medium)** often fits closer to a modern **Small/Slim Medium**. Always compare flat **shoulder-to-shoulder** and **pit-to-pit** measurements against a coat you already own.

## Quick 1970s Suede & Shearling Buying Checklist

1. **Verify Single-Pelt Construction:** Part the wool to confirm natural leather skin underneath rather than woven synthetic backing.
2. **Check High-Tension Areas:** Inspect underarms, collar folds, and buttonholes for cracking or dry rot.
3. **Confirm Period Hardware:** Look for brass **Talon/Scovill** zippers or braided leather buttons.
4. **Account for Wool Thickness:** Size up if you plan to layer heavy knitwear beneath a shearling coat.
5. **Budget for Specialist Cleaning:** Vintage suede and shearling must be cleaned by a leather specialist—never machine-washed or standard dry-cleaned.
