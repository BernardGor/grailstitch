**Vintage 1960s and 1970s M-65 field jackets** are among the most misidentified pieces of American military surplus on the resale market, and separating a \$600 Vietnam-era original from a \$65 commercial replica requires inspecting zipper metallurgy, lining fabric weaves, and `DSA` contract prefixes. Issued to U.S. troops beginning in 1965 in OG-107 olive sateen, the M-65 set the functional standard for American utility outerwear—influencing civilian workwear staples covered in our [1990s Carhartt jacket dating guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-90s-carhartt-jackets). Because Alpha Industries and other government contractors simultaneously manufactured millions of civilian retail jackets alongside military-issue coats, buyers routinely overpay for commercial 1980s replicas listed as "Vietnam War originals."

## How Do You Read the DSA Contract Code and Spot Civilian Alpha Replicas?

The white cotton specification label sewn inside the lower lining (or beneath the collar) provides the exact fiscal year the U.S. Defense Supply Agency ordered the coat:

- **1965–1966 (`DSA-100-XXXX` Without Fiscal Year):** The earliest Vietnam War contracts list `DSA-100-` followed by four or five digits with **no two-digit year code**. These ultra-rare **First Pattern** coats lack shoulder epaulets and feature silver aluminum zippers.
- **1967–1977 (`DSA 100-67` to `DSA 100-77`):** Beginning in **1967**, military contracts inserted the **two-digit fiscal year** directly after `DSA 100-`. For example, a tag stamped **`DSA 100-69-C-1452`** dates to **1969**, while **`DSA 100-74-C-0118`** was contracted in **1974**.
- **1978 and Later (`DLA 100-78`+):** In **1978**, the agency reorganized as the Defense Logistics Agency, shifting the prefix from `DSA` to **`DLA 100-78`** and onward.
- **The Alpha Industries "Three-Bar Logo" Trap:** Genuine military-issue M-65 jackets list a **13-digit NATO Stock Number (NSN)** (such as `8415-00-782-2939`) and a `DSA` or `DLA` contract code, but **never display a commercial brand logo**. When Alpha Industries manufactured commercial M-65s for civilian surplus stores, it added its famous **three-horizontal-bar logo** to the neck tag and omitted the DSA/DLA contract number—an instant tell that a coat is civilian retail rather than military issue.

## Why Did the U.S. Army Replace Silver Aluminum Zippers With Brass in 1972?

Even when fifty years of washing have faded the white specification tag blank, inspecting the **front zipper teeth and collar hood zipper** narrows down the production era immediately—and prevents an expensive zipper repair bill:

- **Silver Aluminum Zippers (1965–1971) & Bottom Pin Wear:** Every genuine 1960s and 1970–1971 Vietnam-era M-65 was built with **matte silver aluminum zippers** supplied by **Conmar**, **Talon**, **Crown**, or **Scovill**. In the field, soldiers discovered two flaws: bare silver aluminum glinted in sunlight, and humid jungle moisture caused galvanic corrosion at the **bottom insertion box and pin**. Always ask sellers for a macro photo of the bottom base of a silver zipper—if the fabric tape near the bottom pin is frayed or separating, the zipper cannot be repaired without replacing the entire track.
- **Heavy Brass Zippers (1972–1980s):** Starting with **1972 contracts (`DSA 100-72`)**, the military phased out aluminum in favor of **heavy-gauge brass zippers** stamped **Scovill**, **General**, or **Serval** (matching the rugged period hardware detailed in our [1960s and 1970s Schott Perfecto guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-1960s-1970s-schott-perfecto-leather-jackets)).

## How Do You Spot a Rare 1965–1966 "First Pattern" M-65?

Collectors pay double to triple standard M-65 prices for **1965–1966 First Pattern** jackets, which are occasionally mislisted by sellers who overlook their subtle structural differences:

1. **Smooth Epaulet-Free Shoulders:** First Pattern M-65 coats have completely clean shoulders with **no buttoned epaulets** (epaulets were added in late 1966/1967 after field feedback showed rifle slings and pack straps slipped off smooth shoulders).
2. **100% Cotton Poplin Interior Lining:** While 1967 and later M-65 jackets switched to a tougher 50% cotton / 50% nylon lining, 1965–1966 First Pattern coats feature a softer, lighter-weight **100% cotton poplin lining** that fades to a distinct pale sage tone.
3. **Silver Aluminum Zippers on Both Front and Collar:** Both the main closure and the concealed collar hood compartment use matching silver aluminum teeth.

## How Do Vintage 1960s and 1970s M-65 Field Jackets Fit?

Because military field jackets were engineered to layer *over* heavy wool field shirts and button-in quilted liners, their cut is substantially roomier than civilian jackets of the same decade (such as the cropped silhouettes covered in our [1960s–1970s Levi’s Type III Trucker guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-1960s-1970s-levis-type-3-trucker-jacket)):

- **Dual Chest & Height Sizing:** Military tags specify both chest breadth (**Small**, **Medium**, **Large**) and torso length (**Short**, **Regular**, **Long**)—for example, **`MEDIUM - REGULAR`** (tailored for a 37-to-41-inch chest).
- **Flat Measurements Benchmark:** A vintage **`Medium - Regular`** M-65 typically measures **22.5 to 23.5 inches pit-to-pit** with a **30-to-31-inch back length**, fitting like a relaxed modern Men’s Large. Most buyers size **down one full size** (choosing a Military Small-Regular or Small-Short) for a sharp everyday fit without a quilted liner.

## Quick 1960s–1970s M-65 Field Jacket Buying Checklist

1. **Verify the Contract Prefix:** Confirm **`DSA-100-`** (1965–1977) or **`DLA 100-78`/`79`** on the white inside specification label—and reject listings showing Alpha Industries’ commercial three-bar logo if you want true military issue.
2. **Check Zipper Alloy & Base Integrity:** Silver aluminum zippers date the jacket to **1965–1971** (inspect the bottom insertion pin for fraying); heavy brass zippers indicate **1972 or later**.
3. **Inspect the Shoulders:** Smooth shoulders with no epaulets identify a rare **1965–1966 First Pattern** M-65.
4. **Confirm Internal Components:** Verify that the concealed nylon collar hood, waist/hem cotton drawstrings, and interior liner buttons are still intact.
5. **Size Down One Size:** Account for military layering room—a tagged **Small-Regular** fits most modern Medium builds cleanly.
