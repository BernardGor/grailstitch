The 1990s gave us chunky silhouettes, visible air units and colorways that still look loud today. They also gave us foam that crumbles after thirty years. Buying a pair is rewarding, but only if you know what to inspect before you pay.

## Check for sole rot first

Polyurethane midsoles absorb moisture and break down over time, a process called hydrolysis. A pair can look perfect in photos and still fall apart on the first wear. Ask the seller to photograph the midsole from the side, and press it with a thumbnail if you can handle the pair in person.

- Powdery residue or a sticky feel means the foam is breaking down.
- Fine cracks along the midsole edge usually spread under load.
- Deadstock pairs are not immune; storage conditions matter more than age.

## Verify authenticity

Compare the tongue label, the font on the size tag and the stitching density against known originals. Production codes inside the shoe should match the era claimed. Be careful with listings that show only stock photos or a single angle.

## Sizing and fit

Lasts changed over the decade, so a size 10 from 1994 may fit differently from a modern 10. Ask for the insole length in centimeters and compare it with a pair you already own.

## What a fair price looks like

Check sold listings rather than asking prices. Wearable pairs in good condition sit well below deadstock, while display-only pairs with crumbling soles should be priced as decor. Factor in shipping and a possible resole.

## The short version

Inspect the midsole, verify the labels, measure the insole and compare sold prices. A careful buyer ends up with a pair worth wearing, not just displaying.
