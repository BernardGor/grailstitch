**Vintage 1960s and 1970s Lee Storm Rider (`101-LJ`) and `101-J` denim jackets** rival Levi’s Type III Truckers in American workwear heritage, yet dating a vintage Lee jacket requires reading completely different construction clues—specifically blanket lining stripe orientation, `MR` trademark symbols, and Union Made chest pocket tags. Introduced in 1933 as the cowboy-cut `101-J` and expanded in 1948 with a warm Alaska wool-blend blanket lining as the **Storm Rider**, Lee jackets were built from left-hand twill Jelt denim that fades to a distinct soft, sky-blue hue compared to the rigid denim covered in our [1960s–1970s Levi’s Type III Trucker guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-1960s-1970s-levis-type-3-trucker-jacket). Because Lee reissued the Storm Rider repeatedly from the 1980s through modern heritage lines, inspecting neck labels and waistband adjusters separates a \$450 1960s original from a \$90 modern reproduction.

## How Do You Read 1960s vs. 1970s Lee Woven Neck Labels?

The black-and-gold woven patch sewn inside the collar (or just below the brown corduroy collar on a Storm Rider) contains three micro-typography shifts that pinpoint the decade:

- **The `MR` Trademark Cutoff (Added Circa 1967–1968):** Early-to-mid 1960s Lee labels display only the registered **`®`** symbol beside the script *Lee* logo. Around **1967–1968**, Lee added a second **`MR`** (Marca Registrada) mark beneath the *Lee* script—so a tag showing **`®`** alone predates ~1968, while a tag with both **`®` and `MR`** dates to **1968 or later**.
- **Lot Number Changes (`101-LJ` vs. `220`):** Through the 1960s and early 1970s, Lee stamped the classic **`101-J`** (unlined) or **`101-LJ`** (lined Storm Rider) lot code directly onto the woven neck label. By the mid-1970s, Lee retired the `101` designation in favor of **`220`** lot codes (such as `220-J` or `220-0141`).
- **"Sanforized" Text Disappears in the 1970s:** 1960s and early-1970s Lee neck labels include the word **`SANFORIZED`** printed in small block letters along the lower border—a detail dropped by the late 1970s.

## Why Does Blanket Lining Stripe Direction Matter on a Vintage Storm Rider?

Flipping open the front placket of a Lee Storm Rider reveals one of the fastest dating clues in vintage denim:

- **Horizontal Stripes (Pre-1964):** Early 1950s and 1960–1963 Storm Rider jackets feature **horizontal wool-blend blanket stripes** running across the width of the torso.
- **Vertical Stripes (1964 and Later):** Beginning around **1964**, Lee rotated the blanket fabric 90 degrees so the multicolored stripes run **vertically from shoulder to hem**—creating a cleaner drape and reducing seam bulk. Any Storm Rider with vertical stripes and no lower side hand-warmer pockets dates squarely to **1964–1983**.
- **Check for Wool Shrinkage & Moth Holes:** Because 1960s Storm Rider linings contain a high percentage of natural wool (unlike synthetic 1980s acrylic linings), improper hot-water washing often shrinks the wool lining slightly tighter than the outer denim shell—causing the bottom hem to pucker upward. Always inspect the lower hem and armpits for lining shrinkage or moth damage.

## How Do Cat-Eye Waist Buttons and Chest Pocket Tags Confirm 1960s–1970s Authenticity?

Before paying collector prices, inspect two small hardware and stitching details that modern reproductions rarely get right:

- **"Cat-Eye" Resin Waist Adjuster Buttons:** Look at the two adjustment tabs along the rear lower waistband—authentic 1960s and early-1970s Lee jackets use dark brown or black **two-hole "cat-eye" buttons** (characterized by a recessed oval groove across the center) rather than flat modern plastic buttons.
- **The *Union Made in U.S.A.* Chest Pocket Ticket:** Reach inside the **left buttoned chest pocket** (marked with the slanted *Lee* pocket tab). Authentic 1960s and 1970s American-made Lee jackets carry a small white cloth **`UNION MADE IN U.S.A.`** label sewn inside the pocket seam—an instant proof of pre-1980s domestic production.
- **Two Pockets Only (Pre-Mid-1980s):** Just like pre-1984 Levi’s Truckers, 1960s and 1970s Lee `101-J` and Storm Rider jackets feature **strictly two buttoned chest pockets** and zero lower slash hand pockets.

## How Do Vintage 1960s and 1970s Lee Denim Jackets Fit?

Lee engineered the `101-J` and Storm Rider specifically for rodeo riders and mechanics, giving them a **distinctly cropped waist and broad shoulders** (similar in torso length to the moto jackets detailed in our [1960s–1970s Schott Perfecto guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-1960s-1970s-schott-perfecto-leather-jackets)):

- **Cropped Belt-Line Length:** A vintage 1960s or 1970s Lee **Size 40 Regular** typically measures **21 to 21.5 inches pit-to-pit** with a cropped **22.5-to-23.5-inch back length**—and lined Storm Rider models feel noticeably snugger inside due to the thick wool blanket lining.
- **Size Up One to Two Sizes:** Most modern buyers need a vintage **Size 42 or 44 Regular** (or a **Long** length tag, such as `42L`, which adds roughly 1.5 inches to the torso and sleeves) for a comfortable modern fit.

## Quick 1960s–1970s Lee Storm Rider & 101-J Buying Checklist

1. **Confirm Two Chest Pockets Only:** Make sure there are **no lower side hand-warmer pockets** (proving a pre-mid-1980s build).
2. **Decode the Neck Tag Symbols:** **`®`** alone = pre-1968; **`®` + `MR`** with `101-LJ`/`101-J` = 1968–early 1970s; **`220`** lot prefix = mid-to-late 1970s.
3. **Check Inside the Left Chest Pocket:** Verify the sewn white **`UNION MADE IN U.S.A.`** cloth ticket.
4. **Inspect Storm Rider Blanket Alignment:** Confirm vertical blanket stripes (1964+) and check that the wool lining hasn't shrunk shorter than the denim outer shell.
5. **Check Waistband Buttons & Length:** Look for recessed **cat-eye** waist buttons and consider a tagged `Long` (`42L`/`44L`) if you prefer extra torso length.
