**Vintage 1990s Stone Island jackets**—designed under Massimo Osti and Paul Harvey—command \$400 to \$2,000+ among European terrace and technical outerwear collectors, and authenticating a genuine 1990s archive piece requires inspecting the `ART` code prefix, sleeve badge border stitching, and button-hole construction rather than trusting a detachable arm patch. During the 1990s, Sportswear Company S.p.A. pioneered garment-dyed fabrics like **Raso Gommato** and **Ice Jacket** thermo-sensitive coatings alongside technical outerwear covered in our [1990s The North Face Nuptse 700 guide](/articles/how-to-authenticate-vintage-90s-north-face-nuptse-700). Because detachable compass badges are routinely swapped onto fake shells—and because counterfeiters flood marketplaces with fake "1990s green-edge" badges—reading the inner care tag is your single most reliable defense.

## How Do You Decode the 8-Digit Stone Island `ART` Number From the 1990s?

Every authentic 1990s Stone Island garment carries a white woven care label sewn inside the lower left seam displaying an **8-digit `ART` (Article) number**—and the first two digits reveal the exact year and season:

- **The Season Math Formula:** Stone Island assigned two-digit season prefixes starting from `01` in 1982. Throughout the **1990s**, the first two digits run from **`13` (Spring/Summer 1990)** to **`31` (Autumn/Winter 1999)**:
  - **`17` / `18`** = Spring/Summer 1992 / Autumn/Winter 1992
  - **`23` / `24`** = Spring/Summer 1995 / Autumn/Winter 1995
  - **`29` / `30`** = Spring/Summer 1998 / Autumn/Winter 1998
  - **`31`** = Autumn/Winter 1999 (the final 1990s season)
- **What the Remaining 6 Digits Mean:** The third and fourth digits (`15`, `40`, `50`) denote the **garment category** (`15` = outerwear/jackets, `50` = knitwear, `20` = shirts/polos), while the final four digits identify the exact fabric and model pattern.
- **No Certilogo QR Tags Prior to 2014:** Stone Island introduced scannable **Certilogo (CLG)** QR tags in **Spring/Summer 2014**. If a seller claims a jacket is from 1996 yet shows a Certilogo tag inside the seam, it is either modern or counterfeit.

## Why Does the "Green-Edge" Badge and Button Test Catch 90% of Fakes?

Because Stone Island badges button onto the left sleeve, scammers frequently attach replica green-edge badges to generic jackets—or pair a genuine 1990s badge with a fake post-2000 coat:

- **The Green-Edge Cutoff (Retired in 2000):** Every authentic Stone Island garment produced between 1982 and **Autumn/Winter 1999 (`ART 31`)** came fitted with a **green-bordered compass badge** (featuring a distinctive forest-green merrowed edge around the black wool-blend patch). Beginning in **Spring/Summer 2000 (`ART 32`)**, Stone Island permanently switched to a **black-bordered badge**. A green-edge badge on an `ART 35` or `ART 42` jacket means someone swapped the patch.
- **Pre-2000 Smooth 4-Hole Buttons vs. Modern "Cross" Buttons:** Until 2000, Stone Island used thick, matte **four-hole buttons** (often rimmed with a subtle raised lip, never a cross). The iconic **raised cross-shaped button** was introduced in **2000** alongside the black-edge badge. If a jacket listed as "1994" has cross-shaped sleeve buttons, walk away immediately.
- **Inspecting the Badge Reverse & Drop-Stitch:** Flip over a genuine 1990s green-edge badge—the back shows crisp **white/cream horizontal bobbin threads** framing the compass and a small vertical "drop stitch" gap where the green border meets itself, never messy black glue or thick cardboard backing.

## What Fabric Degradation Should You Check on 1990s Raso Gommato and Ice Jackets?

Thirty-year-old technical coatings age very differently than standard fleece covered in our [1990s Patagonia Synchilla fleece guide](/articles/how-to-date-vintage-1990s-patagonia-synchilla-fleece):

- **Raso Gommato Polyurethane Peeling:** Iconic 1990s **Raso Gommato** jackets pair a windproof cotton satin exterior with an interior **polyurethane resin coating**. Over 30 years, poor storage or machine washing can cause the inner rubberized coating to turn sticky, flaky, or peel off—always ask sellers for close-up photos of the **inside lining** before bidding.
- **Ice Jacket Thermo-Chromatic Fatigue:** 1990s heat-reactive **Ice Jackets** rely on micro-encapsulated liquid crystals that shift color in cold temperatures; after three decades, many surviving examples remain permanently stuck in their warm-weather shade, which affects collector value.

## How Do Vintage 1990s Stone Island Jackets Fit Compared to Modern Sizing?

1990s Italian Stone Island patterns—especially under Massimo Osti—were cut with **broad, boxy shoulders and shorter waist lengths** compared to modern slim Italian tailoring:

- **Boxy 1990s Cut:** A vintage 1990s Stone Island **Large** typically measures **23.5 to 24.5 inches pit-to-pit** with a relatively short **26-to-27.5-inch back length**, allowing easy layering over heavy knitwear.
- **Measure Flat Dimensions:** Always verify pit-to-pit and collar-to-hem measurements in inches, as modern Stone Island sizing runs roughly one full size trimmer than 1990s archive pieces.

## Quick 1990s Stone Island Authentication Checklist

1. **Read the 8-Digit `ART` Code:** Confirm the first two digits fall between **`13` (1990) and `31` (1999)**—with no post-2014 Certilogo tag.
2. **Match Badge Border to Button Style:** A **green-edge badge** must pair with **pre-2000 smooth 4-hole buttons**, never post-2000 raised cross buttons.
3. **Inspect the Badge Back:** Check for clean white bobbin threads and tight green merrowed border stitching on the reverse of the patch.
4. **Check Inner Coating Condition:** On Raso Gommato and coated cotton pieces, verify that the interior polyurethane membrane is dry and intact rather than sticky or flaking.
5. **Confirm Made in Italy Construction:** Authentic 1990s Stone Island archive outerwear was manufactured in **Italy** with heavy **Lampo**, **Riri**, or **YKK** metal zippers.
