Designed by Irving Schott in 1928 and immortalized in 1950s cinema, the **Schott NYC Perfecto** is the blueprint for every asymmetric motorcycle jacket ever made. Because Schott has produced the **618** (heavy steerhide) and **118** (softer naked cowhide, introduced in the late 1970s) continuously for decades, dating a vintage 1960s or 1970s Perfecto requires looking past the iconic silhouette to examine woven neck tags, zipper manufacturers, and pocket ticket labels.

## 1. Decode the Woven "Perfecto Bull" Neck Label

The woven patch stitched inside the upper back lining is your primary era marker:

- **1950s–Mid-1960s (Pre-Bull Rectangular Labels):** The earliest post-war Schott motorcycle jackets carry a smaller rectangular label reading **`Schott Bros. Sportswear`** or **`Perfecto Genuine Horsehide / Steerhide`** without the illustrated bull graphic.
- **Late 1960s–1970s (The Classic Black & Gold Bull Tag):** By the late 1960s, Schott introduced its famous black woven label depicting a **charging bull** beneath ornate red-and-gold script reading *Perfecto by Schott*. Notably, **1960s and 1970s Bull tags do not have a separate "Made in U.S.A." flag sewn into the bottom seam**—that small white *Made in U.S.A.* tab was added in the 1980s.
- **Inspect the Coin Pocket Ticket:** Reach inside the small snap-flap change pocket on the lower front waist. Vintage 1970s jackets often retain a small white cloth ticket stamped with the **model number (`618` or `613`)** and numeric size (`40`, `42`)—without any modern computer barcode.

## 2. Check the Zippers: Talon and Conmar vs. Modern YKK

Hardware changed significantly at Schott’s New Jersey factory between 1965 and 1985:

- **Talon & Conmar Main Zippers (1960s–Mid-1970s):** Authentic 1960s and early-to-mid 1970s Perfecto jackets feature heavy-duty **Talon** (often stamped *TALON USA* on the pull tab and slider) or **Conmar** front zippers, paired with **ball-and-chain pulls** on the slanted chest pockets.
- **The Transition to YKK & Branded Pulls:** Around the late 1970s and early 1980s, Schott transitioned to heavy-gauge **YKK** and **Ideal** zippers, later introducing custom **Schott**-stamped zipper pulls in the late 1980s.
- **Octagonal vs. Rounded Belt Buckles:** Vintage 1960s and 1970s Perfectos feature an attached front waist belt with a distinct **octagonal (eight-sided) nickel-plated buckle** and star-embossed shoulder epaulets on the **613 "One Star"** model.

## 3. Horsehide vs. Steerhide vs. Naked Cowhide (613, 618, and 118)

Knowing which leather Schott used helps confirm both the model number and decade:

- **Horsehide (`613H` / Early 1960s):** Ultra-dense, glossy, and stiff when unworn; develops dramatic deep-arm creases over decades.
- **Heavy Steerhide (`618` & `613`, 1960s–1970s):** The classic 1960s–1970s Perfecto hide—thick, slightly pebbled, and finished with a protective semi-gloss topcoat that resists rain while developing rich brown undertones ("teacore" wear) along the cuffs and seams.
- **Naked Cowhide (`118`, Introduced Late 1970s):** Uncoated, butter-soft full-grain cowhide introduced around 1977–1978 for riders who wanted immediate comfort without a multi-year break-in period.

## 4. Evaluating Leather Suppleness, Lining Tears, and Sizing

Buying a 50-year-old leather jacket online requires checking two critical condition points before bidding:

- **Hide Flexibility & Dryness:** Ask the seller whether the leather feels **thick and supple** or **stiff, cracked, or flaking** around the collar fold and shoulders. Minor scuffing along the cuffs adds desirable vintage patina, whereas deep surface cracking indicates neglected, dried-out leather.
- **Vintage 1960s–1970s Sizing Runs Small:** A 1970s Schott Perfecto **Size 40** typically measures around **20.5 to 21 inches pit-to-pit** with a cropped **23-to-24-inch back length**—fitting closer to a modern **Men’s Small or Slim Medium**. Most buyers size up one to two numeric sizes from their modern suit size.

## Quick 1960s–1970s Schott Perfecto Buying Checklist

1. **Inspect the Neck Label:** Look for the black-and-gold **Perfecto Bull** tag *without* a sewn-in *Made in U.S.A.* flag tab underneath.
2. **Verify Zipper Stamps:** Confirm **Talon** or **Conmar** markings on the main front zipper slider and ball-and-chain chest pulls.
3. **Check Inside the Coin Pocket:** Look for a small cloth lot ticket (`618` or `613`) with **no barcode**.
4. **Confirm Hide Condition:** Ensure the steerhide remains supple with intact quilted diamond lining and smooth zipper teeth.
5. **Size Up One to Two Sizes:** Measure pit-to-pit carefully, as 1960s–1970s Schott patterns were tailored trimmer across the chest and shoulders than modern fits.
