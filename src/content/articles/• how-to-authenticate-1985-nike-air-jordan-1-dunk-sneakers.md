Few sneakers in history carry the collector mystique—or the price tag—of an original **1985 Nike Air Jordan 1** or **1985–1986 Nike Dunk High ("Be True To Your School")**. Because Nike has reissued both silhouettes hundreds of times since 1994—and because high-grade counterfeits and "aged" customs routinely circulate online—authenticating a true 1980s pair requires examining stamped collar codes, Swoosh proportions, tongue foam construction, and period leather grain rather than trusting a distressed midsole.

## Quick Reference Matrix: 1985 Air Jordan 1 & Dunk vs. Modern Retros

Use this comparison guide to separate genuine 1985–1986 Korean/Taiwanese originals from 1994, 2001, and modern retro releases:

| Feature / Detail | Original 1985–1986 Release | 1994 / 2001 Early Retros | Modern "85 Cut" & Standard Retros |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size Label Location** | **Ink-stamped directly** onto the inside ankle collar lining | Woven tag sewn inside tongue (1994) or inner side wall (2001) | Heat-pressed white size sticker with **UPC barcode** |
| **Collar Code Format** | Size + Year (`85`) + Factory code (e.g., `10 850103 TYPS`) | `940507` (1994) or `010406` (2001) date prefix on tag | `MM/DD/YY` dates + 9-digit SKU (`BQ4422-600`) |
| **Tongue Construction** | Thin nylon with **exposed yellow foam** along top edge | Slightly thicker nylon; stitched top binding (no exposed foam) | Thick padded tongue with enclosed woven binding |
| **Swoosh Shape & Leather** | Larger, sharply hooked Swoosh; stiff board-lasted full-grain leather | Slightly smaller Swoosh; softer tumbled or smooth leather | Variable Swoosh curve; softer modern strobel-board construction |
| **Typical eBay Value** | **$1,500 – $12,000+** (varies heavily by colorway & condition) | $600 – $1,800 | $200 – $550 |

## 1. Read the Stamped Ink Size Code on the Inside Collar

The single most definitive proof of an original **1985 Air Jordan 1** or **1985–1986 Nike Dunk** sits along the inside leather ankle collar—**never on a sewn tongue tag**:

- **No Sewn Size Tag:** Unlike modern sneakers, 1985 Jordan 1s and Dunks do not have a fabric or heat-pressed size label stitched inside the tongue or side lining.
- **Decoding the `YYMMDD` Factory Stamp:** Look inside the upper ankle collar for faded black or blue stamped ink reading something like **`10 850204 TYPS`** or **`9.5 850911 ST-P`**:
  - The first number (`10` or `9.5`) is the **US Men’s size**.
  - The 6-digit string (`850204`) indicates the **1985 production window** (February–April 1985).
  - The 2-to-4-letter suffix (`TYPS`, `TY`, `ST-P`, `BY`, `TH`) identifies the specific **South Korean or Taiwanese factory** that built the shoe. (Note: Factory stamps often vary slightly in font thickness between Korean `TYPS` pairs and Taiwanese `ST-P` pairs, which seasoned collectors cross-check).

## 2. Inspect the Wings Logo, Swoosh Curve, and Exposed Tongue Foam

Because 1985 Jordan 1s were hand-lasted in small Korean and Taiwanese factories, their proportions differ noticeably from modern computer-cut retros:

- **Deeply Embossed Wings Logo:** On 1985 Air Jordan 1s, the **Air Jordan Wings** emblem on the lateral ankle flap is stamped deeply into thick full-grain leather with crisp matte black ink—and the **"TM"** trademark sits cleanly beside the lower right wing tip. Counterfeit 1985 pairs often feature shallow, blurry embossing or incorrect spacing between the letters *AIR JORDAN*.
- **Oversized, Upright Swoosh:** Original 1985 Jordan 1s and Dunks feature a noticeably **larger, fuller Swoosh belly** that curves sharply upward toward the ankle collar compared to 2013–2020 retros.
- **Exposed Open-Cell Tongue Foam:** Look at the top edge of the nylon tongue—authentic 1985 pairs leave a thin strip of **raw polyurethane foam exposed** at the top seam, which naturally oxidizes to a warm orange-yellow hue over forty years.

## 3. Evaluating Midsole Rubber, Collar Crunch, and Wearability

Unlike 1990s Air Max sneakers whose polyurethane midsoles crumble via hydrolysis, **1985 Air Jordan 1 and Dunk midsoles are made of stitched rubber cupsoles**—meaning they do not disintegrate into dust. However, forty-year-old footwear develops two distinct age traits:

- **"Crunchy" Ankle Collar Foam:** The thin padding inside the leather ankle collar of almost every 1985 Jordan 1 or Dunk dries out over decades, making the collar feel stiff or "crunchy" when squeezed. Ironically, slight collar stiffness is a strong sign of genuine 40-year-old foam rather than a modern fake.
- **Hardened Rubber Outsoles:** While a 1985 cupsole won't crumble, the rubber outsole can stiffen and become slick on smooth floors if stored in dry heat. Inspect the heel drag and verify whether the stitching attaching the upper to the rubber midsole remains tight.

## 4. Spotting "Aged" Modern Retros and Fake 1985 Pairs

With original 1985 Chicago and Bred pairs commanding thousands of dollars, sellers and customizers frequently attempt to pass off artificially aged modern pairs as 1985 originals:

1. **Check the Stitch Count & Board Lasting:** Remove the insole (if it isn't glued down) and look at the footbed. Original 1985 pairs are **board-lasted** (showing stiff fiberboard beneath the insole), whereas modern retros use **strobel stitching** (a visible zigzag fabric seam running around the perimeter of the footbed).
2. **Inspect the Lace Eyelets & Toe Box Perforations:** Authentic 1985 pairs have cleanly punched, slightly smaller toe-box ventilation holes and thicker, tighter-weave cotton laces than modern replacements.
3. **Beware of "1985" Box Confusion:** A modern retro box often prints *"1985"* in promotional text or nostalgia graphics, leading casual sellers to list a 2015 or 2020 retro as an original 1985 release. Always verify the **stamped ink collar code** first.

## Quick 1985 Jordan 1 & Dunk Authentication Checklist

1. **Locate the Collar Ink Stamp:** Confirm stamped size, `85`/`86` year code, and factory letters (`TYPS`, `ST-P`, `BY`) inside the ankle collar—with **zero sewn or heat-pressed size tags**.
2. **Check the Footbed Construction:** Lift the insole if loose to verify rigid **board-lasted** construction rather than modern strobel stitching.
3. **Inspect the Tongue Edge:** Look for naturally yellowed **exposed foam** along the top of the nylon tongue.
4. **Verify Wings & Swoosh Geometry:** Ensure deep, sharp Wings embossing and the larger, upright 1985 Swoosh cut.
5. **Assess Collar & Sole Flexibility:** Factor in stiffened ankle collar foam and rubber hardness when evaluating whether a pair is suited for gentle wear or archival display.
